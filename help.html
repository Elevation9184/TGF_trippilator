Start here

Putting it on your phone

  1. Open the link in Chrome. A link tapped inside WhatsApp, Messenger or email opens in that app's own browser, which cannot install it and keeps your ratings somewhere separate. Use ⋮ → Open in Chrome first.
  2. In Chrome, tap ⋮ → Install app. It gets a leaf icon on your home screen.
  3. Open it once while you still have signal. That's when it downloads the 81 gardens and the road distances between them. After that it works in the back blocks with no coverage at all.

Everything you enter — your base, ratings, notes, the gardens you've seen — is kept in your own phone's browser. None of it is uploaded, and nobody else's copy can see it.

Updates

New versions arrive by themselves next time you open the app with signal. The footer shows a build stamp such as build 20261028-1930 — quote it if you report a problem. This guide travels with the app, so it opens with no signal too.

The shape of it

How a day runs

Four tabs, in the order a day actually happens: Nearest · On the way · Map · My day. Under them sits Preselect, which decides what all four are working with.

01

Set your base

Where you're staying. Tap B in the green bar and find it by address. Distances and routes start from here.

02

Choose the gardens you want

In Preselect, or by tapping pins on the Map, or with + Plan on any result. A chosen garden is locked in — it stays in your plan whatever the filters say.

03

Let My day order them

It works out the shortest loop through everything locked in, with driving times and how long the day takes all up.

04

Send it to Google Maps

One tap hands the stops over in order. The app plans; your phone navigates.

05

Tick gardens off as you go

The app notices when you've been somewhere and marks it seen, or you can tap Seen yourself. What's left reshuffles, and the next handoff sends only that.

The two green bar buttons

B and Here

B — your base

Where the group is staying. Find it by typing the address, or press I'm at the base now when you're there. The app then shows it on the map as a dark B, and you can tap the exact spot to correct it — rural addresses land loosely.

When you save it, the app measures the real road distance from your base to all 81 gardens, once, and keeps those numbers on the phone. After that, every plan is accurate offline.

Here — where you are now

One location fix, taken when you press it. Not tracking. It shows its age everywhere it appears, so Here (4 min ago) tells you how much to trust it.

Pressing it once also gives the app permission to follow your day — see My day.

Privacy

Your base is never part of the published app and never leaves your phone. Finding the address and measuring those distances asks OpenStreetMap's free services once, and the app says so before it does.

What's closest, in plain distance order. Use it to fill a gap in the afternoon or to see what's near the place you're standing in.

Each row shows road distance and driving time, which festival it belongs to, its facilities, and a rate box from 1 to 10. Rate gardens as you browse — that's what the ratings ranking runs on, and it takes a second per garden. + Plan locks a garden into the day, Seen? ticks it off, and Map ↗ opens directions to it alone.

Tab two

On the way

For a journey you're already making: New Plymouth down to Hāwera, say. It shows what each garden would add to that drive, rather than how far away it is.

ControlWhat it does
Starting from / Heading toThe two ends of the journey. Both are kept — the run never turns into a round trip by accident.
Detour up toHide anything that would add more than 2, 5, 10 or 20 km.
Order byCheapest detour to see the bargains first, or Driving order to see them in the order you'd pass them.

A row reading +1.2 km means the whole journey gets 1.2 km longer if you stop there. free means it's practically on the way. Tick the ones you want with + Plan, then Send this run to Google Maps.

Tab three

Map

Every garden as a numbered pin, with the coastline, the state highways and the mountain drawn in. It's built from the data already on your phone, so it needs no signal and downloads no tiles.

Pins carry each festival's own map number: C24 for Centuria, F30 for Fringe. A garden in both festivals shows its Centuria number on the map and both in the popup — so someone holding the Fringe brochure can still find it.

An area selection is set all, not toggle each. The first garden the growing box catches decides the direction: if it was in the plan, the box removes; otherwise it adds. Pins preview green or red before you lift your finger.

Pin shading tells you where a garden stands: solid for locked into your plan, plain for available, grey for outside your current filters. Tapping a grey one locks it in anyway — that's how you grab a garden the filters are hiding.

Tab four

My day

The plan itself: your locked-in gardens put into the shortest driving order, with the legs between them, how long each visit is assumed to take, and the total. Up to fifteen gardens it finds the genuinely shortest order, not an approximation.

It follows the day

Once you've pressed Here at least once, My day keeps up with where you are. A garden you're approaching gets an amber border; once you're there it turns green and counts the minutes.

C6 Bee Valley Flower Farm Heading to · 1.2 km
F28 A Moment In Thyme You're here · 11 min · ticks off when you leave
C31 Beechwoods 45 min (assumed) · Seen · remove

Stay ten minutes and drive away, and the garden is ticked off by itself, with a message naming it and an Undo. Drive past without stopping and nothing happens. Where two gardens are next door to each other — C15 Tītoki and C16 Te Rongohua are 16A and 16B Kinross Drive — it asks which one you're at rather than guessing.

If you undo an automatic tick, the app stops ticking things off until your plan changes. It assumes it got something wrong and stays out of the way.

Worth knowing

While Google Maps is in front, the app can't see your location at all — no web app can. It catches up the moment you switch back to it, which is usually as you park. If you'd rather not rely on it, turn Tick off as I go off and use the Seen button on each stop.

Marking a garden seen doesn't reshuffle your afternoon. It stays in the list struck through as done ✓, so the route keeps its shape, and only the gardens still to go are sent to Google Maps.

Google Maps takes ten stops at a time. A longer day is sent in batches — tick gardens off as you go and the button offers the rest.

The control room

Preselect: Filters and Gardens

Preselect decides what every tab is working with. On a phone it opens as two panes, Filters and Gardens; on a tablet or laptop they sit side by side.

The one idea to hold on to: ticking a garden locks it in, and a locked-in garden is immune to every filter. Filters decide what you're browsing; locking in decides what you're doing.

Gardens

The list you tick. At the top sits an estimate of the day as it stands: the shortest loop through everything locked in, with hours including the visits.

  • Search starts at two letters and matches name, street, area or garden number. Numbers match whole, so C1 finds C1 and not C10 to C19. A search also lists matching gardens that your filters are hiding, in a separate section underneath — so you can find a garden by name and lock it in without undoing anything.
  • Choose all and Release all act on what's currently shown. Search for "Hāwera", press Choose all, and you've locked in exactly those.
  • A garden you untick stays in view marked released until you next change a filter — room to change your mind without hunting for it again.
Your own judgements

Garden Edit

Next to Preselect. Same two-pane shape: a list of gardens on the left, the fields on the right. This is where you record what you think, on one garden or on a dozen at once.

Ticking a garden here highlights it for editing. It does nothing to your plan. That's the difference between the two screens, and it's deliberate: rating six gardens shouldn't quietly add them to your day.

Names, addresses, coordinates, facilities and opening days come from the festivals and can't be edited. For a single garden they're shown underneath as From the festival listing.

Editing several at once: a field shows its value where the highlighted gardens agree, and mixed where they don't. Changing one field touches that field only, on the gardens where it makes a difference — rating five gardens leaves their notes alone. A blank box over mixed values means leave them as they are, which is why clearing has its own buttons.

Changes save as you make them, so there's no Save button to forget in a car park. Undo says what it will undo — Undo: rating 8, 3 gardens — and goes back through your changes one at a time until you reload the page.

Keeping your work

Backing up, and moving to another phone

Your ratings, notes and visits live in this browser on this phone, and nowhere else. That's what keeps them private, and it's also the risk: clearing Chrome's site data wipes them.

Export notes (CSV) at the bottom of the page hands you a spreadsheet of all 81 gardens through the normal Android share sheet, so you can email it to yourself, drop it in Drive, or send it on. Import notes reads one back — that's how you move to a new phone, or how ratings gathered at the kitchen table reach everyone else.

The spreadsheet opens in Excel or Sheets, so rating gardens at a desk works too. Only the columns you've actually filled in are read back; anything unrecognised is ignored rather than written somewhere odd.

Reality check

If something looks wrong

What you seeWhat's happening
Google Maps shows a different house numberThe app sends the festival's own pin, and Maps labels it with the nearest street address. Two gardens are known to disagree with their printed address — Winterfell at F41 (59 Gisborne Terrace, pin 167 m up the road) and The Village Gallery in Eltham. Trust the signage on the day.
days ? against a gardenThat festival doesn't publish opening days per garden, so the app won't guess. Unknown is never treated as closed — but check before a long drive.
A list is emptier than expectedRead the filter summary under the tabs. Rated at least leaves out anything you haven't rated, which catches everyone once.
Nothing at all in NearestNo starting point yet. Set B, or press Here.
The day isn't following youPress Here once to allow location. The app never asks on its own.
Distances look approximateThey're measured road distances between known gardens. From a spot the app hasn't measured — a fresh base, or a fix in the middle of nowhere — it estimates, and says which it used.
Reporting anything odd

Send the build stamp from the footer, which tab you were on, and what you expected. That's usually enough to find it.
